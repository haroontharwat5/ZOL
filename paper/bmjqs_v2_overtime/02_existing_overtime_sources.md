# Existing References — Those Relevant to the Overtime Story

These are references already verified ([V]) in the prior drafts (`paper/06_literature_review.md` and `paper/bmjqs/03_reference_strategy.md`). They carry over to the overtime paper. Each entry notes exactly which overtime claim it supports.

---

## Core: overtime as the binding OR cost

1. **Dexter F, Abouleish AE, Epstein RH, Whitten CW, Lubarsky DA. (2004).** Use of operating room information system data to predict the impact of reducing turnover times on staffing costs. *Anesth Analg* 97(4):1119–26.
   **Used for:** The foundational argument that overtime is the binding cost in OR operations, weighted 1.5–2× the cost of idle time. Grounds our decision to focus on overtime rather than utilisation.

2. **McIntosh C, Dexter F, Epstein RH. (2006).** The impact of service-specific staffing, case scheduling, turnovers, and first-case starts on anesthesia group and operating room productivity. *Anesth Analg* 103(6):1499–516.
   **Used for:** Overtime as the primary operational and economic KPI in OR management. (Already referenced in prior drafts.)

3. **Dexter F, Epstein RH. (2009).** Typical savings from each minute reduction in tardy first case of the day starts. *Anesth Analg* 108(4):1262–7.
   **Used for:** Establishes the FCOTS cost claim ($3–4/minute) that we will test against our data. In the overtime paper, this is the reference we rebut with our cascading finding.

4. **Wachtel RE, Dexter F. (2009).** Tactical increases in operating room block time for capacity planning should not be based on utilization. *Anesth Analg* 108(4):1215–20.
   **Used for:** Shows first-case tardiness propagates through the day — i.e., cascading as a recognised mechanism. Directly supports our Genk cascading finding.

## Cascading and behavioural response near boundaries

5. **Fügener A, Schiffels S, Kolisch R. (2017).** Overutilization and underutilization of operating rooms: insights from behavioral health care operations management. *Health Care Manag Sci* 20(1):115–28.
   **Used for:** Surgeons adjust pace near block boundaries. Explains why our cascaded cases finish slightly shorter than planned even after a near-six-hour start delay (mean duration diff = −22 min).

6. **Pandit JJ, Abbott T, Pandit M, Kapila A, Abraham R. (2012).** Is "starting on time" useful (or useless) as a surrogate measure for "surgical theatre efficiency"? *Anaesthesia* 67(8):823–32.
   **Used for:** R² = 0.04–0.08 between case start and case finish times — first-case punctuality explains only a tiny fraction of end-time variance. Matches our Genk pattern where the worst-late-start room (GEG1, 90 % late) has zero overtime, and the worst-overtime room (GO10, 32.9 %) is mid-pack on punctuality.

## Duration estimation as overtime driver

7. **Strum DP, May JH, Vargas LG. (2000).** Modeling the uncertainty of surgical procedure times: comparison of lognormal and normal models. *Anesthesiology* 92(4):1160–7.
   **Used for:** Statistical foundation for the right-skewed duration distributions we see in Genk (P99 = 411 min; P99.9 = 630 min). Justifies our CV-by-bucket decomposition.

8. **Eijkemans MJ, van Houdenhoven M, Nguyen T, Steyerberg EW, Habbema JDF, Kazemier G. (2010).** Predicting the unpredictable: a new prediction model for operating room times. *Anesthesiology* 112(1):41–9.
   **Used for:** Surgeon estimate dominates duration prediction; scheduling buffers explain why most cases finish early. Relevant to our Genk finding that 54.3 % of cases finish shorter than planned.

## Definitions and measurement framework

9. **Bauer M, Diemer M, Merkel M, Schrader T, Schuster M, Wulf H. (2020).** Glossary of perioperative process times and indicators. *Anaesthesist* 69(Suppl 1):S5–17.
   **Used for:** Definitions of overtime minutes, turnover time, start-time deviation. We cite for methodological consistency.

## Benchmarking context (lighter role in new paper)

10. **Schouten AM, Flipse SM, van Nieuwenhuizen KE, Jansen FW, van der Eijk AC, van den Dobbelsteen JJ. (2023).** Operating room performance optimization metrics: a systematic review. *J Med Syst* 47(1):19.
    **Used for:** 47 different OR metrics with inconsistent definitions — justifies why we pick overtime specifically and define it transparently.

11. **Macario A. (2006).** Are your hospital operating rooms "efficient"? *Anesthesiology* 105(2):237–40.
    **Used for:** Historical context for OR scorecard. Less central than before; cited briefly.

## Safety and handover precedents already in our drafts

12. **Zhang C, Pandit JJ. (2023).** Getting operating theatre metrics right to underpin quality improvement. *Br J Anaesth* 131(1):130–4.
    **Used for:** Ties overtime metrics to quality improvement framing. Still useful because Zhang & Pandit explicitly link metric misuse to safety decisions.

13. **Abdelfattah E, et al. (2020).** Workflow disruptions and outcomes. *BMJ Qual Saf* 29:1009–17.
    **Used for:** Published in BMJ Q&S. Links OR flow disruption to patient outcomes — the editorial template we are fitting.

14. **Joseph A, et al. (2019).** Minor flow disruptions, traffic-related factors, and their effect on major flow disruptions. *BMJ Qual Saf* 28(4):276–83.
    **Used for:** Published in BMJ Q&S. Cascading of minor disruptions into major ones — a direct parallel to our 4,786 shift-displaced cases arising from mid-day delay accumulation.

## References from prior drafts that do NOT carry over

These were load-bearing for the cross-campus argument. In the Genk-only paper they either vanish or appear only in a one-sentence methodological footnote.

- **Korzhenevich G, Zander A. (2024).** German OR benchmarking dataset. Now only relevant as the closest prior quantitative work on OR overtime, if we want a brief literature-positioning line. Not central.
- **Ernst C, et al. (2012).** Single-layer case-mix adjustment. Only appears if we discuss why a within-site analysis is cleaner than adjusted between-site comparison.
- **Zhang C, Dunstan C, Pandit JJ. (2024).** Capped utilisation critique. Keep if we note that static utilisation targets miss the concentration pattern we document.
- **Baumgart A, et al. (2017).** Benchmarking data alone didn't help. Only relevant if we argue that data collection without within-site room-level focus misses the problem.
- **Merlo J, et al. (2006).** Multilevel analysis tutorial. Not needed in the Genk-only paper unless we add a multilevel model for room-level variance.
- **Ligthart-Melis GC, et al. (2022).** Dutch between-site variation. Not needed — we dropped cross-site.

## Summary

Of 17 references in the earlier drafts, 14 remain useful for the overtime paper. The 9 core overtime references (Dexter 2004, McIntosh 2006, Dexter & Epstein 2009, Wachtel & Dexter 2009, Fügener 2017, Pandit 2012, Strum 2000, Eijkemans 2010, Bauer 2020) plus the two BMJ Q&S precedents (Abdelfattah 2020, Joseph 2019) form the existing-literature foundation.
