# Pivot Decision — From Cross-Campus Comparison to Overtime at Genk

**Date:** 2026-04-14
**Owner:** Haroon (with Niels, Maxim)
**Next meeting:** early May 2026

---

## What we are changing

The earlier draft (in `paper/` and `paper/bmjqs/`) built its story around a four-campus comparison. The core move was a progressive stratification ladder that filtered raw overtime rates through four adjustment layers, with the between-site spread narrowing from 16.8-fold to 3-4-fold. The discussion argued that most of the raw gap was structural, and that multi-site OR benchmarks therefore mislead.

Niels pushed back on this framing in the April meeting. The objection was not about the numbers, which are clean, but about the shape of the argument. A four-campus comparison invites the first reviewer question that will sink it: are these campuses actually comparable? Even with shared governance, shared EHR, and shared labor agreements, the reviewer can always respond that a 24/7 tertiary center, an ambulatory day-only site, a hybrid campus, and a cath lab serve different populations, carry different case mixes, and have different staffing contracts in practice. The entire paper then has to defend the comparison before the findings can land. That is a hard hill to climb in a BMJ QS cover letter.

The alternative Niels proposed is narrower and sharper. One site — Genk — one concept — overtime — and a double argument that connects the operational finding to both staff harm and patient harm. The quantitative work stays mostly the same. The framing collapses from a methodological statement about benchmarking into a concrete question: where does overtime at a high-volume tertiary OR actually come from, and who pays the cost?

## What stays

- All Genk data: 96,044 cases, January 2022 to May 2025
- The within-site room-level overtime analysis (GO10 at 32.9%, GEG1 at 0%, and everything between)
- The cascading analysis (4,786 cases in a different shift than planned, mean delay 352 minutes)
- The urgent–elective overlap pattern (69.7% of days)
- Duration estimation variability by duration bucket
- Start-time delay patterns
- The descriptive backbone from the in-depth Genk PDF

## What goes

- Progressive stratification as the methodological centerpiece
- The 16.8-fold → 3-4-fold narrative
- Measurement validity as the organizing frame
- Cross-campus tables as primary findings

Cross-campus numbers can still appear in one compact comparison table (Table 26 in the in-depth file) for context — Genk 8.4% vs Cathlab 5.8% vs Maaseik 2.8% vs Lanaken 0.5%. But the table earns its place as background, not as the main event.

## New central claim

At a high-volume tertiary OR (Genk), one in twelve cases runs past the scheduled shift end. The cases that spill are not random. They cluster in a small set of rooms, and most of the minutes they add reflect mid-day cascading rather than late starts or individual overruns. Overtime concentrated this way carries two kinds of cost. Staff bear a disproportionate share of the fatigue, schedule unpredictability, and work-family spillover. Patients whose cases cross shift boundaries are exposed to fatigued teams and handover transitions that the safety literature identifies as error-prone. A data-driven understanding of where overtime comes from is therefore a prerequisite for any serious intervention on either side.

## Why BMJ QS still fits

The paper no longer leans on measurement validity. Instead it sits squarely inside what BMJ QS publishes on OR topics: a safety-relevant operational pattern, documented with rigor at scale, linked to outcomes through cited evidence. The closest precedents remain Abdelfattah et al. (2020) and Joseph et al. (2019), both of which treat OR flow as a safety question. Our paper fits the same template, with a cleaner and more focused claim than the cross-campus version.

## Fallback

If BMJ QS returns a desk rejection on scope, the same Genk-centered paper fits:
- BMJ Open Quality (lower bar, QI focus)
- Anaesthesia (overtime as workforce concern)
- BJA (if we lean clinical-operational)
- HCMS (if we lean quantitative-operational)

The Genk-only framing is actually a better fit for each of these fallbacks than the cross-campus version was.
