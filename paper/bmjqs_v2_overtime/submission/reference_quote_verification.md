# Reference Quote Verification

For each reference: our claim, the exact verbatim quote from the source that supports it, and a match verdict. Quotes were recovered from PubMed records, publisher abstracts, and indexed full-text snippets. Full-text PDFs were behind institutional access, so quotes marked (snippet) are reconstructed from search-engine-indexed abstract text and should be eyeballed against the PDF if you have access.

## Ref 1 — Wachtel & Dexter 2010 (Anesth Analg 110(6):1698-1710) — MATCH
**Claim:** Overutilised time costs approximately twice as much as underutilised time.
**Verbatim (abstract):** "Planning too little staffing causes overutilized time, which is approximately twice as expensive as underutilized time."
**Verdict:** Direct match. The phrase is in the abstract.

## Ref 2 — Griffiths 2014 (Med Care 52(11):975-81) — MATCH
**Claim:** Overtime and long shifts linked to poorer perceived quality of care and higher patient safety risk.
**Verbatim (abstract, Results):** "Working overtime was also associated with reports of poor or failing patient safety (OR=1.67; 95% CI, 1.51-1.86), poor/fair quality of care (OR=1.32; 95% CI, 1.23-1.42), and more care left undone (RR=1.29; 95% CI, 1.27-1.31)."
**Verdict:** Match, if anything understated. (Sample is 31,627 nurses across 488 hospitals — our table says "33,659"; see issues list.)

## Ref 3 — Dall'Ora 2015 (BMJ Open 5(9):e008331) — MATCH (table number to fix)

**Verbatim (abstract):** Emotional exhaustion aOR 1.26 (95% CI 1.09-1.46); intention to leave aOR 1.29 (95% CI 1.12-1.48).
**Verdict:** Body claim correct. **Supplementary table error:** we wrote "intent to leave (OR 1.13)" — the paper reports **1.29**, not 1.13. Also "burnout (OR 1.26)" is specifically the emotional-exhaustion subscale.

## Ref 5 — Dall'Ora 2016 (Int J Nurs Stud 57:12-27) — MATCH (table number to fix)

**Verbatim (abstract, Results):** "shifts of 12 h or longer are associated with jeopardised outcomes... working overtime was associated with decreased job performance."

## Ref 6 — Dall'Ora 2020 (Hum Resour Health 18:41) — PARTIAL
**Claim:** The combination of high workload and low decision latitude is an established burnout predictor.
**Verbatim (full text):** Lists "high workload, value incongruence, low control over the job, low decision latitude, poor social climate/social support, and low rewards" as predictors of burnout. Conclusion: "adverse job characteristics — high workload, low staffing levels, long shifts, and low control — are associated with burnout in nursing."
**Verdict:** Partial. Both factors appear as predictors but are listed separately (Maslach framing), not as a combined demand-control interaction. Our "combination" framing is an interpretation. Defensible, but if challenged, the demand-control combination is Karasek's model, not this paper's claim.

## Ref 7 — Cortegiani 2020 (Br J Anaesth 124(5):623-37) — MATCH
**Claim:** Meta-analysis pooling 18 studies, adjusted OR 1.16 (95% CI 1.06-1.28), low-certainty.
**Verbatim (abstract, Results):** "Primary analysis from adjusted estimates demonstrated an association between night/after-hours surgery and a higher risk of mortality (odds ratio [OR]=1.16; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.06-1.28; P=0.002; number of studies=18; I²=67%) based on low certainty evidence."
**Verdict:** All four numbers confirmed exactly.

## Ref 8 — Oh & Song 2025 (Anaesthesia, DOI 10.1111/anae.16559) — MATCH (add "90-day")
**Claim:** 281,717 propensity-matched patients; odds ratio 3.58.
**Verbatim (abstract):** "after a 1:5 propensity score matching, 281,717 were included... Patients in the after-hours group showed 3.58-fold (OR 3.58, 95%CI 3.47-3.69, p < 0.001) and 2.51-fold (hazard ratio 2.51, 95%CI 2.46-2.57) higher 90-day and 1-year all-cause mortality rates, respectively."
**Verdict:** Both numbers exact. **Recommend specifying "90-day mortality"** — the OR 3.58 is for 90-day mortality; the 1-year figure is HR 2.51 (not an OR).

## Ref 9 — Sakurai & Takeda 2025 (Anaesthesia 80(5):596-7) — MATCH

Text: even after matching, a four-fold higher rate of emergency procedures persisted after-hours, "suggesting that residual confounding by emergency status may persist despite the propensity score adjustment."
**Verdict:** Match. It is a correspondence letter critiquing Oh on confounding.

## Ref 12 — Pittman 2025 (JAMA Netw Open 8(4):e252875) — MATCH
**Claim:** Overtime above a breakpoint threshold associated with 2.09% increase in pressure ulcers across 70 US hospitals.
**Verbatim (abstract/Findings):** "Exceeding breakpoint thresholds was associated with a 6.44% increase in pressure ulcers for agency nurse hours and a 2.09% increase for overtime hours." Study used "data from 70 US hospitals, from January 2019 through December 2022."
**Verdict:** Match. 2.09% is specifically for overtime (agency hours = 6.44%); our claim correctly attributes it to overtime.

## Ref 13 — Althoff 2021 (BMJ Qual Saf 30(8):678-688) — MATCH
**Claim:** >350,000 non-cardiac cases; night surgery associated with increased morbidity (adjusted OR 1.41), mediated partly by transfusion rates and provider handovers.
**Verbatim (abstract):** "night surgery was associated with increased morbidity (ORadj 1.41, 95% CI 1.33 to 1.48, p<0.001)." Mediation: "The effect was mediated by potentially preventable factors, including higher blood transfusion rates and more frequent provider handovers." Sample 350,235.
**Verdict:** Match. OR 1.41 confirmed (it is the secondary outcome; primary outcome mortality was OR 1.26). Paper's mediation measured anaesthesia handovers; its conclusion uses "provider handovers" — our wording is fine.

## Ref 16 — Zhang, Dunstan & Pandit 2024 (Anesthesiol Perioper Sci 2(4)) — PARTIAL
**Claim:** Aggregate/capped utilisation metrics hide room-level operational problems.
**Verbatim (abstract):** The Model Hospital "prefers a 'capped' measure, in which any touch time occurring in late finishes is ignored." Conclusion: capped theatre utilisation "is not a suitable metric for operating theatre performance... its underlying algorithm produces aberrant values."
**Verdict:** Partial. Strongly supports "capped/aggregate utilisation is a flawed, distorting metric." But the abstract's emphasis is the metric's internal flaws, not specifically "hides room-level variation beneath a site aggregate." If cited for "aggregate utilisation can distort operational reality," well supported; the exact "hides room-level problems" phrasing is our framing.

## Ref 20 — Strum 2000 (Anesthesiology 92(4):1160-7) — MATCH
**Claim:** Surgical duration follows a log-normal distribution; CV varies by procedure type (basis for CV analysis).
**Verbatim (abstract):** "The Shapiro-Wilk test indicates that the log-normal model is superior to the normal model for a large and diverse set of surgeries."
**Verdict:** Match for the log-normal basis. The paper does not use the phrase "coefficient of variation" — CV is the mathematical consequence of log-normality. Defensible.

## Ref 21 — Eijkemans 2010 (Anesthesiology 112(1):41-9) — MATCH (wording caveat)

**Verbatim (abstract):** "The surgeon's estimate had an independent and substantial contribution to the prediction, and the final model explained 27% of the residual variation in log (OR time)." Consider rephrasing the supplementary-table wording to "the surgeon's estimate adds independent predictive value beyond historical means, yet most duration variability remains unexplained."
