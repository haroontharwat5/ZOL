# Reference verification report

Verified 2 May 2026 by web search of PubMed, journal pages, and the issuing body for HSSIB. Each reference was checked for (a) bibliographic correctness and (b) whether the specific claim made in the paper draft matches what the source actually reports.

Status legend:
- **CLEAN** — bibliographic details correct and claim matches source.
- **AUTHOR/CITATION ERROR** — paper exists but cited authors, journal, volume, or pages are wrong.
- **CLAIM MISMATCH** — paper exists but our paper attributes a number, finding, or framing that the source does not actually contain.
- **CLAIM OVERSTATED** — paper exists but the wording in our paper goes beyond what the source supports.

## Patient-harm and after-hours mortality

### Ref 6 — Cortegiani A et al. *Br J Anaesth* 2020;124(5):623-37 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 32147102. DOI 10.1016/j.bja.2020.01.019.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32147102/
- Quote from paper: *"the primary analysis from adjusted estimates demonstrated an association between night/after-hours surgery and a higher risk of mortality (odds ratio [OR]=1.16; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.06-1.28; P=0.002; number of studies=18; I2=67%) based on low certainty evidence."*
- Our claim "adjusted odds ratio of 1.16 (95% CI 1.06 to 1.28)" matches.
- **Caveat to add to the draft:** the meta-analysis labels the underlying evidence as low-certainty (GRADE). A cautious one-sentence acknowledgement would strengthen the claim.

### Ref 7 — Oh TK, Song IA. *Anaesthesia* 2025 — **AUTHOR/CITATION ERROR**
- DOI 10.1111/anae.16559.
- Link: https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/anae.16559
- The paper has TWO authors: Oh TK and Song IA. "Jo Y-Y" should be removed from our reference list.
- Quote from paper: *"after a 1:5 propensity score matching, 281,717 were included (57,497 in the after-hours group and 224,220 in the in-hours group)."*
- Our claim "281,717-patient propensity-matched cohort from South Korea" matches.
- **Important contextual issue:** The paper reports OR 3.58 for 90-day mortality, much larger than Cortegiani's pooled OR 1.16. Sakurai (Ref 26) attributes this to inadequate confounder control given a 4-fold imbalance in emergency procedures even after matching. The draft should not present Oh 2025 as a clean confirmation — pair it with the critique.

### Ref 8 — Saager L et al. *Anesthesiology* 2014;121(4):695-706 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 25102312.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25102312/
- Quote: *"incidence of 8.8, 11.6, 14.2, 17.0, and 21.2% for patients with 0, 1, 2, 3, and ≥4 transitions; odds ratio 1.08 [95% CI, 1.05 to 1.10] for an increase of 1 transition category."*
- Our claim "8.8% at zero transitions to 21.2% at four or more" matches exactly.
- **Caveat:** A follow-up Cleveland Clinic study (Terekhov et al. 2016, PMID 27466034) reached the opposite conclusion. We can keep Saager as the primary citation but the discussion of handover risk should not read as settled.

### Ref 26 — Sakurai T. *Anaesthesia* 2025 — **CLEAN**
- DOI 10.1111/anae.16591.
- Link: https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/anae.16591
- Correspondence challenging Oh 2025 on confounding: "even after adjustment there was a four-fold higher rate of emergency procedures after-hours (4.1% vs. 16.0%)."
- Matches our description as a critique.

### Ref 27 — Guerra-Londono JJ et al. *J Surg Oncol* 2025 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 39388390. DOI 10.1002/jso.27941. Volume 131(3), pp.457-464.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388390/
- Quote: *"Ninety-day major complications occurred in 35 (21.6%) of TH and 96 (21.9%) of AH patients. With adjustment of other covariates, no significant association was found between AH or TH and 90-day major complications."*
- Matches our claim of no association in HPB surgery.

### Ref 28 — Starmer AJ et al. *N Engl J Med* 2014;371(19):1803-12 — **CLEAN, with context note**
- PMID 25372088. DOI 10.1056/NEJMsa1405556.
- Link: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556
- Quote: *"Preventable adverse events (injuries due to medical errors) decreased by 30 percent—from 4.7 to 3.3 errors per 100 admissions."*
- Matches our 30% claim.
- **Caveat:** I-PASS is a paediatric inpatient resident-handoff study, not an intraoperative anaesthesia handover study. The current draft cites it as evidence that structured handovers can reduce adverse events generally, which is fine — but reviewers may prefer an explicit setting note.

## Staff harm and burnout

### Ref 3 — Griffiths P et al. *Med Care* 2014;52(11):975-81 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 25226543. PMC4196798. DOI 10.1097/MLR.0000000000000233.
- Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4196798/
- Quote: *"Working overtime was also associated with reports of poor or failing patient safety (OR=1.67; 95% CI, 1.51-1.86) and poor/fair quality of care (OR=1.32; 95% CI, 1.23-1.42), as well as more care left undone."*
- Matches our claim.

### Ref 4 — Dall'Ora C et al. *BMJ Open* 2015;5(9):e008331 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 26359284. PMC4577950. DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2015-008331.
- Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4577950/
- Quote: *"Nurses working shifts of >=12 h were more likely than nurses working shorter hours (<=8) to experience burnout … and report intention to leave their job due to dissatisfaction."*
- Matches our claim.

### Ref 5 — Bae S-H. *Int J Public Health* 2024 — **AUTHOR/CITATION ERROR + CLAIM MISMATCH**
- PMID 38746597. DOI 10.3389/ijph.2024.1607068.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38746597/
- Single author: Sung-Heui Bae (Ewha Womans University, Seoul). Our citation "Cho (Bae) J, Cho HJ, et al." is wrong — there is no co-author.
- Setting: SOUTH KOREAN acute-care hospitals, not US. Our draft says "397 US nurses" — both the country and the n are wrong. The analytic n is 264 (across 28 South Korean hospitals); 397 was the initial sample.
- The mandatory-overtime → intent-to-leave finding does match.
- **Fix:** rewrite the citation to a single-author Bae and correct the country and n.

### Ref 23 — Dall'Ora C et al. *Int J Nurs Stud* 2016;57:12-27 — **CLAIM MISMATCH**
- PMID 26952077. DOI 10.1016/j.ijnurstu.2016.01.007.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26952077/
- The paper exists but the verifier could not locate a verbatim sentence supporting the specific framing "unplanned overtime is more harmful than planned long shifts." The review treats overtime and long shifts as separate-but-overlapping risk factors.
- **Fix:** either source the specific contrast from the full PDF or soften the claim to "both long shifts and overtime are associated with worse performance and wellbeing."

### Ref 24 — Dall'Ora C et al. *Hum Resour Health* 2020;18:41 — **CLAIM MISMATCH (minor)**
- PMID 32503559. PMC7273381. DOI 10.1186/s12960-020-00469-9.
- Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7273381/
- Quote: *"We identified high workload, value incongruence, low control over the job, low decision latitude, poor social climate/social support, and low rewards as predictors of burnout."*
- Our paper says "unpredictable workload as burnout driver." The paper says "high workload" and "low control / low decision latitude." The framing is close but not identical.
- **Fix:** rewrite to "high workload and low control over the job are associated with burnout."

### Ref 25 — Van Bogaert P et al. *BMC Nurs* 2017;16:5 — **CLAIM MISMATCH (minor)**
- PMID 28115912. PMC5241948. DOI 10.1186/s12912-016-0200-4.
- Link: https://bmcnurs.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12912-016-0200-4
- Quote: *"Nurse management at the unit level and workload had a direct impact on outcome variables."*
- The setting is two acute-care university hospitals in Antwerp (Belgium), not a "Flemish nursing workforce" in any population-representative sense. The paper uses "decision latitude," not "schedule control."
- **Fix:** describe as "two Belgian university hospitals" and use "workload, unit-level nurse management, and decision latitude" instead of "workload and schedule control."

### Ref 9 — HSSIB. UK 2025 — **CLEAN, with provenance note**
- Link: https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/the-impact-of-staff-fatigue-on-patient-safety/investigation-report/
- Quote: *"A survey of doctors who were members of the Medical Defence Union in 2025 (481 respondents) found that 22% of respondents felt sleep deprived on a daily basis and a further 19% on a weekly basis."* And: *"35% of doctors said tiredness had impaired their ability to treat patients."*
- Numbers (22%, 35%) are correctly cited.
- **Provenance note:** the figures come from an MDU member survey of 481 doctors embedded inside the HSSIB report. The HSSIB themselves did not collect those numbers. A precise citation would say "an MDU member survey of 481 doctors cited in the 2025 HSSIB national patient-safety investigation."

### Ref 10 — Pittman P et al. *JAMA Netw Open* 2025 — **CLAIM OVERSTATED**
- PMID 40172888. DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.2875.
- Link: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2832111
- Quote: *"Exceeding breakpoint thresholds was associated with a 6.44% increase in pressure ulcers for agency nurse hours and a 2.09% increase for overtime hours."* The other patient-safety indicators tested showed no significant association with overtime.
- Our claim "directly associated with patient-safety indicators across 70 US hospitals" is too sweeping. The clean overtime signal is in pressure ulcers only; the larger effect was for agency hours.
- **Fix:** rewrite to "Overtime hours above a breakpoint threshold were associated with a 2.09% increase in pressure ulcers across 70 US hospitals; the agency-staffing signal was larger (6.44%)."

## Operating-room flow and BMJ QS precedents

### Ref 21 — Joseph A et al. *BMJ Qual Saf* 2019;28(4):276-83 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 30158119. DOI 10.1136/bmjqs-2018-007957.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30158119/
- Quote: *"Studies in operating rooms show that minor disruptions tend to group together to result in serious adverse events such as surgical errors."*
- Matches our framing.

### Ref 22 — Koch A et al. *BMJ Qual Saf* 2020;29(12):1033-45 — **AUTHOR/CITATION ERROR + CLAIM OVERSTATED**
- The cited "Abdelfattah" is not the correct author. Correct paper: Koch A, Burns J, Catchpole K, Weigl M. "Associations of workflow disruptions in the operating room with surgical outcomes: a systematic review and narrative synthesis." *BMJ Qual Saf* 2020;29(12):1033-1045 (not 1009-17). PMID 32447319. DOI 10.1136/bmjqs-2019-010639.
- Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32447319/
- Quote: *"On average, 20.5% of operating time was attributed to flow disruptions and these disruptions were either negatively or not substantially associated with surgical outcomes."*
- Our claim "linked flow disruptions directly to outcomes" overstates this systematic review, which found mixed/null associations.
- **Fix:** correct authors, journal pages, and tone down the claim to something like: "A systematic review of OR workflow disruptions estimated that ~20% of operating time involves disruptions, with mixed evidence of direct linkage to surgical outcomes."

## Fatigue and shift-length evidence

### Ref 29 — Barger LK et al. *PLoS Med* 2006;3(12):e487 — **CLAIM MISATTRIBUTED**
- PMID 17194188. DOI 10.1371/journal.pmed.0030487.
- Link: https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.0030487
- The "36% increase in serious medical errors" attribution is WRONG. Barger 2006's actual findings are odds ratios: *"during months in which between one and four extended-duration shifts and five or more extended-duration shifts were worked, the odds ratios of reporting at least one fatigue-related significant medical error were 3.5 (95% CI, 3.3–3.7) and 7.5 (95% CI, 7.2–7.8), respectively."*
- The 36% figure originates in Landrigan 2004 (our Ref 30), not in Barger 2006.
- **Fix:** either (a) attribute the 36% claim to Landrigan 2004 only and remove the Barger citation here, or (b) restate Barger as "Shifts exceeding 24 hours were associated with 3.5- to 7.5-fold higher odds of fatigue-related significant medical errors."

### Ref 30 — Landrigan CP et al. *N Engl J Med* 2004;351(18):1838-48 — **CLEAN**
- PMID 15509817. DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa041406.
- Link: https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa041406
- Quote: *"Interns made 35.9 percent more serious medical errors on the traditional schedule than on the intervention schedule."*
- This is where the "36%" figure actually comes from.
- **Fix linked to Ref 29:** since the 36% statistic belongs here, the current "shorter intern shifts reduced serious errors by a similar magnitude" sentence in the draft becomes circular. Consolidate the two statements.

### Ref 31 — Gates M et al. *BMJ Open* 2018;8(9):e021967 — **CLAIM OVERSTATED**
- PMID 30244211. DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2018-021967.
- Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6157562/
- Quote: *"21 studies showed no association with surgical performance, and mixed findings for psychomotor performance, work performance and medical errors."* Conclusion: *"fatigue and insufficient sleep may be associated with negative physician health outcomes."*
- Our paper says the review "confirmed these patterns across physician and patient outcomes." That is too strong. The review found a clearer signal for physician health than for patient outcomes.
- **Fix:** rewrite to "A systematic review of physician fatigue confirmed associations with physician health outcomes, with mixed evidence for direct effects on surgical patient outcomes."
